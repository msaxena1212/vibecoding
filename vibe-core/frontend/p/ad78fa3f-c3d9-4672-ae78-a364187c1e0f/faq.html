Frequently Asked Questions

We primarily source our data from reputable government agencies such as the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) and the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC). We also integrate data from internationally recognized databases like EDGAR (Emissions Database for Global Atmospheric Research) and Climate Watch (World Resources Institute - WRI). For specific estimations where real-time granular data is unavailable, we utilize scientifically validated emission factors and models, adapted to the Indian context, often referencing IPCC guidelines and academic research. Our commitment is to transparency; we detail our sources and methodologies in the 'About Us' section.

India's major emissions stem from several key sectors. The energy sector, heavily reliant on coal, is a primary source of Carbon Dioxide (CO2). Industry (manufacturing, cement, steel) also contributes significantly to CO2 and other pollutants. Transport is a major source of NOx, CO, and PM2.5. Agriculture contributes Methane (CH4) and Nitrous Oxide (N2O), while waste management (landfills) is a significant source of CH4. Residential fuel burning and industrial processes also release various pollutants. We track CO2, CH4, N2O, SO2, NOx, PM2.5, CO, and VOCs.

Our emission calculations primarily follow the standard methodology of multiplying activity data by relevant emission factors. For instance, calculating CO2 emissions from a power plant would involve the amount of coal burned (activity data) multiplied by the CO2 emission factor for coal combustion. For sectors like transport, we use data on vehicle types, fuel consumed, and distance traveled. We leverage a curated database of emission factors specific to Indian conditions, sourced from official reports and scientific literature. For broader trends and where specific data is limited, we utilize aggregated data from sources like EDGAR and Climate Watch.

The '5-second Clarity Rule' is a user experience principle we adhere to. It means that within the first five seconds of landing on any page, a user should intuitively understand the purpose and value proposition of that page or the application as a whole. For our platform, this translates to clear headlines, concise subheadings, and immediately understandable data visualizations that convey the critical nature of emission data for India.

The availability of real-time data varies significantly by pollutant and source. While some air quality monitoring stations (like those managed by CPCB) provide near real-time data for pollutants like PM2.5 and SO2, comprehensive real-time emission data specifically categorized by source (e.g., individual industrial stacks, specific vehicle models) is often not publicly available in India. Our platform provides the most up-to-date data accessible from official sources and uses robust estimation methodologies for other areas. We clearly indicate the source and timeliness of the data presented.
